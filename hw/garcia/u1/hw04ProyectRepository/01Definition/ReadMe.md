# **Problem Definition** 

# **Problem:** 

The ice cream shop is currently struggling to manage its operational flow due to issues related to product information. This leads to data loss, difficulty tracking  the status of available products and increased time spent on administrative tasks. Furthermore, without a centralized system, it is difficult to maintain proper control over the shop’s daily operations and to access the necessary information to make decisions. We need a system that allows the ice cream shop to manage its products, customers, orders and sales. The system should allow employees to update products, calculate the total price of each order and keep track of sales. 

# **Overview:** 

The project involves developing a simple application to support the management of the ice cream shop by centralizing the information needed to streamline operations. The proposal aims to reduce issues caused by manual information handling and provide a more organized and efficient way to manage the business. 

The goal is to reduce manual record keeping,therefore, the solution will be designed to provide a simple tool tailored to the needs of an artisanal ice cream shop. 

# **BackGround:** 

The ice cream shop is a business dedicated to producing ice cream products and offering a variety of options. In its daily operations, the business must manage information regarding its products, customers, orders and sales 

Currently, several of these processes are carried out manually, using physical records that are not centralized into a single tool. This type of management may be suitable for a small business in its early stages. However, as operations, sales, the volume of information, and the number of customers grow, maintaining organized and efficient control becomes difficult. 

Therefore, a need arises for a software solution to support the management of the ice cream parlor's activities. Implementing an application would help organize information to improve business processes, serving as a tool to control workflows.
